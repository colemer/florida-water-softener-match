Hard water in Tampa: what's different here

Tampa's water is among the hardest in this guide: the city reported 12.3 grains per gallon for 2025, and its main source is the Hillsborough River rather than wells alone.

  • City of Tampa water averaged 211 mg/L total hardness in 2025, or 12.3 grains per gallon, at pH 7.88.[4]
  • The Hillsborough River is Tampa's primary water source, with supplemental supply bought from Tampa Bay Water.[4]

Who issues permits in Tampa?

City of Tampa Construction Services (inside city limits); Hillsborough County for unincorporated addresses. Phone: 813-274-3100, option 1. Construction Services is at 2555 E. Hanna Avenue. Tree permits are reviewed by Natural Resources foresters or a City of Tampa Self-Certified Private Arborist.[5][6]

Florida law exempts installation and maintenance of water conditioning units by water conditioning operators from contractor and plumber licensing, but separate plumbing or electrical work (for example a new outlet or re-piping) is regulated. Ask the office whether your install needs a permit.

What does it cost?

Angi's 2026 cost guide puts the U.S. average for water softener installation at about $1,500, with a reported range of roughly $200 to $6,000 depending on system type, size and plumbing.[7] These are national figures; we have not found public local cost data.

Tampa water softener FAQs

How hard is City of Tampa water?

The 2025 report puts average total hardness at 211 mg/L, which the city converts to 12.3 grains per gallon. Use that as a starting softener setting, then confirm with a tap test.[4]

Where does Tampa's tap water come from?

The Hillsborough River is the primary source, treated at the city plant with ozone, and Tampa Bay Water supplies additional water.[4]

Does the river source change from season to season?

Surface water quality moves with rainfall more than deep wells do, and the city also blends in Tampa Bay Water supply. That is one reason to test at your tap instead of relying only on a yearly average.[4]

I live in unincorporated Hillsborough. Does this apply?

Not necessarily. The figures above are for City of Tampa Water customers. Many Tampa mailing addresses are served by Hillsborough County utilities, so check the name on your water bill.[4]

What to look for in a Tampa water treatment company

Four checks before you sign anything. How our matching works.

Check 1 · Licensing

Know Florida's licensing rule

  • Florida exempts water-conditioning installers from contractor licensing, and local governments may not require them to be licensed as plumbers
  • So ask about insurance, years in business and service response times instead
  • Ask for a water test before you get a quote; many utilities publish hardness (JEA, for example, lists it by ZIP)

Source[8][9]

Check 2 · Insurance

Proof of insurance

  • Ask for a current certificate of general liability insurance in the company's name
  • Ask whether the crew working at your home is covered by workers' compensation
  • Call the insurer on the certificate to confirm the policy is active
