Hard water in Fernandina Beach: what's different here

Fernandina Beach treats its six-well supply with aeration and chlorine only, and JEA serves other parts of Amelia Island at about 16 grains per gallon. Which number applies depends on who bills you.

  • JEA also serves parts of Amelia Island; it lists ZIP 32034 at 273 ppm, about 16 grains per gallon, for its own customers.[5]

Who issues permits in Fernandina Beach?

City of Fernandina Beach Building Department. Phone: 904-310-3135. Inside city limits, apply to the city (204 Ash St.; online or buildingpermits@fbfl.city). Amelia Island addresses outside the city are permitted by Nassau County.[6]

Florida law exempts installation and maintenance of water conditioning units by water conditioning operators from contractor and plumber licensing, but separate plumbing or electrical work (for example a new outlet or re-piping) is regulated. Ask the office whether your install needs a permit.

What to look for in a Fernandina Beach water treatment company

Four checks before you sign anything. How our matching works.

Check 1 · Licensing

Know Florida's licensing rule

  • Florida exempts water-conditioning installers from contractor licensing, and local governments may not require them to be licensed as plumbers
  • So ask about insurance, years in business and service response times instead
  • Ask for a water test before you get a quote; many utilities publish hardness (JEA, for example, lists it by ZIP)

Source[8][5]

Check 2 · Insurance

Proof of insurance

  • Ask for a current certificate of general liability insurance in the company's name
  • Ask whether the crew working at your home is covered by workers' compensation
  • Call the insurer on the certificate to confirm the policy is active

General guidance

Check 3 · Estimate

A written, itemized estimate

  • Scope, materials and quantities in writing, not just a total
  • Who applies for any permit, and who pays the permit fee
  • Start date, expected timeline, cleanup and haul-away

Check 4 · Payment

Fair payment terms and a warranty

  • Be wary of paying most of the price before work starts
  • Tie payments to finished stages of the job
  • Get the workmanship and product warranties in writing
