Hard water in St. Augustine: what's different here

St. Augustine is one of the few places in the region where the city plant already runs reverse osmosis, so a softener quote here should start with a tap test, not a sales sheet. Addresses just outside city limits can be on a different system with different water.

  • The City of St. Augustine draws from deep wells in the Upper Floridan aquifer and treats with reverse osmosis, filtration, aeration and free chlorine; the plant is permitted for 6.5 million gallons a day and serves over 17,000 customers.[5]
  • The city's treatment page does not publish a hardness number, so test at the tap. Addresses with a St. Augustine mailing address outside city limits may be on St. Johns County Utility, whose CR 214 Mainland plant also uses reverse osmosis; JEA customers in 32095 (north St. Augustine) are listed at 355 ppm, about 21 grains per gallon.[5][6][7]

Who issues permits in St. Augustine?

City of St. Augustine Planning & Building Department. Inside city limits, permits come from the city, not the county. In the historic districts, exterior changes need a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Architectural Review Board (HARB) before a building permit can be issued (HARB staff: 904-209-4326). Addresses with a St. Augustine mailing address outside city limits (for example St. Augustine Shores) go to St. Johns County Building Services.[8][9][10][11]

Florida law exempts installation and maintenance of water conditioning units by water conditioning operators from contractor and plumber licensing, but separate plumbing or electrical work (for example a new outlet or re-piping) is regulated. Ask the office whether your install needs a permit.

What does it cost?

Angi's 2026 cost guide puts the U.S. average for water softener installation at about $1,500, with a reported range of roughly $200 to $6,000 depending on system type, size and plumbing.[12] These are national figures; we have not found public local cost data.
