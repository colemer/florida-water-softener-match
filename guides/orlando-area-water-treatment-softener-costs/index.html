Orlando-area tap water: treatment, sodium and softener costs (2026)

Last updated November 21, 2026 · Written for Florida homeowners by Florida Water Softener Match

Short answer

Short answer: Most Orlando-area cities pump Floridan Aquifer water and treat it for hydrogen sulfide (ozone in Winter Park, aeration in Oviedo, Toho and Winter Garden). Several of their latest reports do not publish a hardness number, so test your tap before buying. Angi's 2026 national average installed cost for a softener is about $1,500.

Winter Park and Lake Mary

Winter Park pumps lower Floridan water from six wells up to 1,330 feet deep and uses ozone to remove hydrogen sulfide; sodium tested at 10.5 ppm.

Lake Mary treats six upper Floridan wells at one plant with UV advanced oxidation, hydrogen peroxide, granular activated carbon and chlorine; sodium tested at 10.3 ppm.[1][2]

Oviedo and Sanford

Oviedo uses 10 Floridan wells with forced-draft aeration and chloramine disinfection; its 2024 report lists no hardness figure.

Sanford draws from 17 Floridan wells, chlorinates and adds polyphosphate for corrosion control; sodium tested up to 38.3 ppm, higher than most systems in this guide.[3][4]

Kissimmee (Toho) and Winter Garden

Toho Water Authority's Central system draws Floridan water recharged on the Lake Wales Ridge and treats mainly by aeration; sodium up to 9.9 ppm, no hardness figure in the 2025 report.

Winter Garden's six deep Floridan wells are treated by aeration and sodium hypochlorite; sodium 10.0 to 21.0 ppm, no hardness figure listed.[5][6]

What it costs

Angi's 2026 national installed costs: about $1,500 on average; single-tank $500 to $1,700, double-tank $1,000 to $5,000, salt-free $800 to $4,000, one-tap RO $1,500 to $1,800, whole-home RO $4,000 to $11,000. Because Oviedo, Toho and Winter Garden do not publish hardness, ask for a written water test with any quote.[7]

Cost ranges

Angi's 2026 cost guide puts the U.S. average for water softener installation at about $1,500, with a reported range of roughly $200 to $6,000 depending on system type, size and plumbing.[7]

Frequently asked questions

Why does Orlando-area tap water smell like sulfur?

The Floridan Aquifer water many cities use contains naturally occurring hydrogen sulfide; Winter Park removes it with ozone, and Toho and Winter Garden mainly with aeration.[1][5][6]

Will a softener add sodium?

Ion-exchange softeners can add some sodium. If you watch sodium, note your starting level: Sanford's water tested up to 38.3 ppm, Winter Park's 10.5 ppm.[4][1]
