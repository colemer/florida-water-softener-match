Northeast Florida water hardness by utility and ZIP (2026)

Last updated November 21, 2026 · Written for Florida homeowners by Florida Water Softener Match

Short answer

Short answer: Most Northeast Florida tap water comes from the Floridan Aquifer and is hard to very hard. JEA's published averages run from about 7 grains per gallon (120 ppm) in 32065 and 32234 to 22 grains per gallon (372 ppm) in 32081. Atlantic Beach reports 18 grains per gallon. Some systems, including the City of St. Augustine and St. Johns County's CR 214 Mainland plant, use reverse osmosis.

  2. City-run systems

JEA (by ZIP code)

JEA lists hardness by ZIP for its own service territory, averaged across the wells feeding each plant and sampled October to December of the prior year. Examples: 32081 Nocatee 372 ppm (22 gpg); 32082 Ponte Vedra Beach 346 ppm (20 gpg); 32095 north St. Augustine 355 ppm (21 gpg); 32092 World Golf Village 307 ppm (18 gpg); 32259 Fruit Cove 305 ppm (18 gpg); 32250 Jacksonville Beach area 292 ppm (17 gpg); 32233 Atlantic Beach/Mayport area 266 ppm (16 gpg); 32034 Amelia Island area 273 ppm (16 gpg); 32073 Orange Park 128 ppm (8 gpg); 32065 120 ppm (7 gpg). JEA classifies anything over 180 ppm as very hard.

These numbers apply only if JEA is your water provider. Several beach cities, the Town of Orange Park, Fernandina Beach, Clay County and St. Johns County run their own systems.[1]

City-run systems

City of Atlantic Beach: 300 mg/L, which the city converts to 18 grains per gallon for softener settings.

City of Jacksonville Beach: water comes from artesian wells 900 to 1,200 feet into the Floridan Aquifer; the city asks residents to call its Water Plant (904-247-6278) for hardness.

City of St. Augustine: deep wells in the Upper Floridan aquifer, treated with reverse osmosis, filtration, aeration and chlorine.

City of Fernandina Beach: six Floridan Aquifer wells, treated by aeration and chlorination.

Town of Orange Park: four wells in the Upper Floridan Aquifer.[2][3][4][5][6]

County utilities

Clay County Utility Authority: Floridan Aquifer groundwater, aerated for odor and chlorinated; its report describes no softening step.

St. Johns County Utility, CR 214 Mainland system: eight Floridan wells, reverse osmosis treatment, aeration and chloramination.[7][8]

Frequently asked questions

Is hard water a health problem?

JEA says using a softener or filter is a personal choice that is mainly about aesthetics, not health, and notes ion exchange can raise sodium.[1]
